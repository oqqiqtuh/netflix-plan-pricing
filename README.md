## Adding someone who doesn't live with you

An extra member costs **$7.99/month with ads or $9.99/month without ads**, per person. Each one gets their own login and password, but the bill goes to whoever invited them, and your base plan decides how many slots you have.

Here the arithmetic gets interesting. Standard plus one ad-free extra member is $19.99 + $9.99 = **$29.98/month**, which is $2.99 more than Premium at $26.99. Paying more for less sounds like a bad trade, but the two options solve different problems.

Premium's four streams must all be inside the same household. An extra member exists precisely because someone is outside it. So:

- **Three or four people, all at one address:** Premium at $26.99 is cheaper than Standard plus an ad-free extra member at $29.98, and you get 4K as a bonus.
- **One person at a different address:** the extra member slot is the only legitimate way to include them, at $9.99 ad-free or $7.99 with ads on top of your plan.
- **Two people at one address, nobody elsewhere:** Standard at $19.99 stays the cheapest ad-free option.

If you subscribed through a package or a third-party add-on, extra member eligibility and pricing can differ from the figures above.

## The household rule nobody reads until it bites

Netflix defines your household as the collection of devices connected to the internet at the main residence where you watch Netflix. Sharing the account with anyone outside that household isn't allowed; those people are supposed to become extra members.

In practice this rule lands hardest on people with two addresses. A student in a dorm, divorced parents whose kids move between homes, someone with a second property, and anyone who travels for weeks at a time all run into location checks that a single-address household never sees. NerdWallet ran an AI-assisted review of Reddit threads on Netflix, with the caveat that posts are anonymous and individual situations can't be verified, and location-based access came up as a recurring complaint from exactly those groups. The same review found people describing the ad-supported tier as "not that bothersome" and praising the breadth of the catalog, while others felt Netflix cancels shows too early.

One practical detail: profiles and viewing history travel with the account, so if you downgrade or upgrade, your list doesn't reset.

## Cancelling, pausing, and the contract question

There is no long-term contract. Netflix membership is month-to-month from the moment you sign up, and you can cancel online in two clicks with no cancellation fee.

If you're leaving temporarily rather than for good, NerdWallet notes that customers paying by credit or debit card can pause the membership for one month at a time, up to three months in total. That keeps your profiles and watch history intact instead of starting over. Pausing isn't available on every payment method, so check the option in your account before you cancel outright.

Costs to keep in mind around the billing date: charges are monthly on the date you signed up, and depending on where you live, taxes may be added on top of the membership price. Cancelling mid-cycle doesn't produce a partial refund, so time it for the day before your renewal if you want the full value of the month you already paid.

## Common questions

**Is there an annual plan that works out cheaper?**
No. Netflix states it doesn't offer annual, multi-month, or lifetime plans. Your only option is monthly billing, so a year costs 12 times the monthly price.

**Can I still sign up for Basic?**
No, Basic has been discontinued. The cheapest plan now is Standard with ads at $8.99.

**What exactly do I lose on the ad-supported plan?**
Commercials, plus a minority of titles that show a lock icon because they aren't licensed for the ad tier. Netflix says all games and most movies and TV shows are available, and it doesn't publish a list of what's excluded.

**Do the prices include tax?**
No. The $8.99 to $26.99 range is pre-tax, and local taxes may be added depending on where you live.

**How many extra members can I add?**
One on Standard, up to two on Premium, at $7.99 each with ads or $9.99 each without. Standard with ads doesn't include extra member slots.

## Which plan to pick

Match the plan to how many people watch and where they live, not to how much content you think you'll get through.

- **One person, occasional viewing:** Standard with ads at $8.99. You give up ad-free viewing and a slice of the catalog, and you save $11 a month against Standard.
- **Two people at the same address who hate commercials:** Standard at $19.99. Two streams covers a couple watching different things, and 1080p is fine on most TVs under 55 inches.
- **Four people at one address, or anyone with a 4K set:** Premium at $26.99. Four streams plus 4K and HDR, and it undercuts Standard plus an ad-free extra member.
- **Someone outside your household needs access:** price the extra member at $9.99 and compare the total against Premium before you commit.

Two things to settle before you enter card details. First, your billing date becomes the day you sign up, so pick a date that sits comfortably in your budget cycle. Second, if you'll regularly watch from a second address, read the household definition again; that's the restriction most likely to interrupt your viewing a few months in, and no plan tier avoids it.
